About this dashboard

Crowd Counting Consortium · Phase 3 · January 2025 onward

The Crowd Counting Consortium (CCC) is a joint project of Harvard Kennedy School and the University of Connecticut that collects publicly available data on political protest events reported in the United States, including rallies, marches, demonstrations, and acts of civil disobedience. This dashboard lets you explore that record: where events happened, when, what they were about, and how many people took part.

CCC draws on a broad, continually updated set of sources: an automated crawl of print and broadcast media; regular review of social media accounts run by organizers, activists and independent journalists, which often surface events commercial outlets miss entirely; Google News searches to catch what the crawler doesn't; periodic review of organizers' own websites and calendars; and public submissions through CCC's online form, added only once the event is confirmed to have taken place.

Filters apply together, and the map, the figures above it and the charts on the right update as one. Drag the handles under the date range to narrow the period. Numbered circles group nearby events — click one to zoom in. When the ring around one turns teal, every event inside it shares one exact location (including the territory panels along the bottom), so zooming further won't separate them — click to open a list instead. Individual points are coloured by political valence and sized by crowd size; hover one for its date, size, claim and sources. The Legend button in the map's top-right corner explains these symbols.

Reading the numbers

  • Crowd size is recorded for about 30% of events, and not at random — larger, more widely covered gatherings are far more likely to have an estimate. The participant total sums only those events; the coverage figure beside it shows what share of the current selection it rests on.
  • Events are located to their city or town, not a street address, so many events in the same place share identical coordinates.
  • Absence of events isn't evidence of absence. The record depends on what the sources above surfaced. Know of something missing or wrong? CCC accepts corrections through the submission form above.

Getting the data

The full dataset and its codebook are published openly on Harvard Dataverse, along with the earlier phases covering 2017–2024, which are not shown here.

If you use the data in published work, please cite the Crowd Counting Consortium as the source. Each dataset version on Dataverse carries its own DOI — see the Dataverse page for the one matching the version you used.
